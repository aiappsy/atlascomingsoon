## 3. Topic 2: The Airline Revolution — Duffel NDC vs. Legacy GDS
### The Problem with Public Flight Booking
In commercial aviation, the public has long believed that flight ticket prices are identical everywhere. However, traditional travel agencies and public flight search engines rely on ancient distribution infrastructure called GDS (Global Distribution Systems—such as Amadeus, Sabre, and Travelport).
- For every ticket booked via legacy GDS, legacy networks extract high distribution surcharges.
- Major airlines (including British Airways, Lufthansa Group, SAS, Air France/KLM, and Emirates) have introduced **GDS penalty surcharges ranging from $15 to $35 per ticket** on public retail channels to penalize bookings made through legacy pipes.
- Public OTAs also heavily mark up ancillary services: charging a **30% to 50% premium on checked luggage and seat selection**.

### The Solution: Direct Carrier API Integration via Duffel NDC
Airlines have unified under IATA’s **NDC (New Distribution Capability)** standard. NDC allows airlines to sell directly to approved technology partners via modern APIs, bypassing legacy GDS surcharges entirely.
- Through **Duffel**, Atlas connects directly to the NDC gateways of over 300 global airlines.
- **Zero Booking Fees:** Atlas members pay the raw airline net fare without the $20–$45 retail agency markup.
- **Continuous Pricing:** Airlines release exclusive, dynamic floating price points directly through NDC that rigid public search engines cannot display.
- **At-Cost Luggage & Ancillaries:** Members book checked bags and premium seat selection at the airline’s exact net cost, saving $30 to $70 per traveler on baggage alone.
- **Realistic, Honest Savings:** Typical savings are **8% to 14%** on standard economy flights and **18% to 26%** on long-haul business class fares.
